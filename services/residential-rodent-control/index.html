Pre-WWII crawl-space homes (1900-1945)

High risk, Norway rat + mice
  • Original foundation vent screens deteriorated or missing
  • Sill-plate gaps from 80+ years of wood movement
  • Pipe penetrations from mid-century plumbing upgrades
  • Under-porch and under-addition crawl spaces often unvented

Postwar ranch & bungalow (1945-1970)

Moderate to high, all species
  • Crawl-space or slab-on-grade depending on lot
  • Original aluminum vent screens now corroding
  • Garage service doors with worn sweeps
  • Utility penetrations from HVAC retrofits

Canopy-adjacent any era (Irving Park, Fisher Park, Sunset Hills)

High risk, roof rat primary
  • Gable-vent screens aged or missing entirely
  • Soffit-return gaps from decades of wood expansion
  • Tree limbs contacting or overhanging roofline
  • Ridge vent gaps from roofing upgrades

Newer suburban (1980-present)

Lower risk, seasonal mice
  • Tighter build standards reduce frame gaps
  • Field-mouse pressure at suburban-agricultural edge
  • Garage door gaps still common entry point
  • Utility conduit penetrations at foundation

Frequently asked

Common questions

Does home construction type affect which rodents I'm likely to have?

Yes, a lot. Crawl-space homes, common in central Greensboro neighborhoods like Aycock, Westerwood. College Hill, are the primary target for Norway rats, which enter through foundation vent gaps, pipe penetrations. Sill-plate gaps. Homes under heavy hardwood canopy, especially in Irving Park, Fisher Park. Sunset Hills, face roof rat pressure still of foundation type, roof rats enter at the roofline. Slab-on-grade homes have fewer entry points at the foundation but face Norway rat pressure through utility-line penetrations and garage thresholds. Ranch homes with combined crawl-space and attic access face potential pressure from both species.

What's the right time of year to treat for rodents in a Greensboro home?

Any time, because Greensboro's humid subtropical climate means rodent breeding continues year-round with no hard winter pause. That said, fall (September-December) is the highest-pressure season: roof rats migrate from cooling canopy into attics. Norway rats increase indoor activity as outdoor food sources decline. House mice seek warm structures. Inspecting and sealing in late summer (August-September) before the fall pressure spike is the best prevention strategy. If you already have an active infestation, treat immediately still of season.

Will rodent treatment require me to leave my home?

No. Our home jobs don't needs you to vacate. Snap traps are placed in inaccessible areas, inside cabinets, behind appliances, in crawl spaces, where they pose no household risk. Outside bait stations are tamper-resistant housings placed outside the living envelope. The only area we'd ask you to keep family and pets away from temporarily is the crawl space during and immediately after job.

How long does a residential rodent program take from first call to clearance?

An early-detection home call, one rodent, clear entry point, limited activity. Can be fully resolved in 2-3 weeks: same-day or next-day initial visit, 10-14 day follow-up. Clearance. An established infestation requiring exclusion sealing and multiple follow-ups usually runs 3-5 weeks from first visit to confirmed clearance. We don't close a home job until the follow-up confirms no new activity.

For residential rodent control, the quote is shaped mainly by the species involved, how many entry points show up, and how hard the affected areas are to reach.
