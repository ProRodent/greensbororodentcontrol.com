Frequently asked

Common questions

What counts as a rodent emergency?

A rodent emergency is any situation where the infestation has moved into occupied living space. A rat or mouse in a bedroom, kitchen. Living room where it poses an immediate safety or hygiene risk. It also includes dead-rodent situations where the carcass is producing a strong odor and is inaccessible (wall cavities, attic, crawl space). Business situations like a restaurant that can't open until the rodent issue is resolved for a health inspections.

How fast can you respond to an emergency rodent call in Greensboro?

For most Greensboro and Guilford County addresses, we can dispatch a technician within 2-4 hours of your call during daytime hours. Evening and weekend calls are usually same-day or early next morning depending on exact timing. Call (844) 635-0403 directly, our emergency line is staffed 24/7. We'll give you a realistic ETA for your specific address.

There's a dead rat in my wall, how do you find and remove it?

Dead-rodent location uses a combination of odor concentration mapping (the smell peaks near the carcass), moisture-meter readings to detect decomposition in wall cavities. Tactile probing of open cavities. Once located, we access the carcass through the least-invasive opening possible, sometimes a vent opening or access panel is sufficient. Sometimes a small access hole is needed. After extraction, the area is treated with an antimicrobial deodorizer and the access is sealed.

Is it safe to stay in my home during an emergency rodent situation?

A rat or mouse in a living area is unpleasant but usually not an immediate evacuation-level risk. Keep children and pets away from the area where rodent activity is concentrated, don't handle rodents or droppings with bare hands. Avoid disturbing nesting areas until a technician arrives. For dead-rodent situations with strong odor, ventilating the space and temporarily avoiding the affected room is reasonable while you wait for extraction.

Will the emergency service also seal the entry point?

We always aim to identify and, where possible, seal the primary entry point during the emergency visit. In some cases, mostly complex attic roof-rat situations, full exclusion sealing is a separate scheduled visit after the population is eliminated. We won't leave a job without at least a temporary seal on any confirmed active entry point.
