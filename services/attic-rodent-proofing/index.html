Frequently asked

Common questions

Does attic rodent proofing affect ventilation?

Not when done correctly. The goal is to seal entry points while preserving the ventilation function that those openings provide. At gable vents, we install 1/4-inch hardware-cloth frames that allow full airflow while blocking rodent entry. At ridge vents, we install internal baffle screening that maintains the ridge's ventilation channel without closing it. Soffit-return gaps are sealed with flashing rather than plugging the soffit vent itself. Improper attic rodent proofing that blocks ventilation creates moisture and heat problems that can be as damaging as rodent activity, we're careful to avoid that.

What's the most common attic rodent entry point in Greensboro?

For most Greensboro homes, the gable vent is the primary entry, mostly on pre-1970 homes with original wood-frame gable vents that have deteriorated screens. The second most common is the soffit-return gap at eave ends. Where the soffit panel meets the fascia board and a separation has developed over decades of wood movement. On homes with ridge vents installed during roofing upgrades, improperly screened ridge vents are increasingly common as a third entry vector.

Should I proof the attic before or after treating the rat infestation?

After, always. Attic rodent proofing must follow confirmed population clearance. If gable vents and soffits are sealed while roof rats are still active in the attic. The sealed population has no exit, dies inside the attic. Produces weeks of decomposition odor in the living space below. The correct sequence: attic trap network → confirmed clearance (no new trap strikes for 5+ days) → exclusion sealing → follow-up verification.

Do squirrels and bats use the same attic entry points as roof rats?

Yes. Gable vents, soffit gaps. Ridge openings are used by multiple attic-dwelling species, roof rats, gray squirrels, and sometimes little brown bats. Our attic proofing work seals entry points against all three. For bat colonies, but, NC law governs timing of exclusion. Bats may not be excluded during the maternity season (May 15-August 1 in North Carolina) when pups are flightless. If we observe bat activity during an attic inspections, we note it and discuss the timing requirements with you before any exclusion work near known bat roosts.
