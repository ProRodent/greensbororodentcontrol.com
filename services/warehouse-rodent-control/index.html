Frequently asked

Common questions

Why are loading docks the primary rodent entry point in Greensboro warehouses?

Loading docks create a unique rodent-entry spot: dock plates and dock levelers leave steady gaps at floor level. Dock doors are opened repeatedly and left open during extended loading work, dock bumpers age and separate from the foundation. Dock-side dumpsters and spilled product create harborage and food sources immediately adjacent to the entry point. Norway rats that colonize dock-side areas have frictionless access to the building inside when dock doors open. Sealing the dock perimeter, dock-door brush seals, dock-plate gaskets. Dock bumper gap closure, is the single most effective warehouse rodent prevention measure.

How do rodents get into pallet storage areas in Greensboro warehouses?

Pallet storage creates ideal rodent harborage, elevated platforms for roof rats and enclosed floor-level cavities for Norway rats. Stacked pallets provide nesting sites protected from foot traffic and mechanical gear. Rodents enter pallet storage areas through dock entries, loading bays left open after receiving. Through floor-level gaps at the base of fire-door frames and roll-up door thresholds. Product stored directly on pallets that touch the ground or walls is mostly vulnerable. A standard warehouse rodent protocol says 18-inch clearance from walls and 12-inch elevation on product storage.

What monitoring documentation do you provide for warehouse accounts?

Warehouse checking programs include a bait-station map showing station placement and numbering. A service log updated after each visit with station-by-station bait consumption and activity notation, a quarterly trend report flagging stations with elevated activity. Product data sheets and SDS for all rodenticides in use. For facilities with food product or FDA-regulated material, we can provide records formatted to support third-party food safety audits.

How many bait stations does a Greensboro warehouse need?

Station count depends on perimeter length, dock count, and pressure level. A general guideline for warehouse facilities is one tamper-resistant outside station per 50-75 linear feet of perimeter. Plus additional stations at every dock entry and any vegetation or storm-drain adjacency. A 50,000 sq ft warehouse with a standard rectangular footprint and 4 dock doors might needs 20-30 outside stations. We provide a site-specific station count with justification after an perimeter walkthrough.
