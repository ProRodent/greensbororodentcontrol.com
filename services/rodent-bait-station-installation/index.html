Frequently asked

Common questions

What is a tamper-resistant bait station and why does it matter?

A tamper-resistant bait station is an EPA-mandated housing for second-generation anticoagulant rodenticides. Federal law needs these housings for any bait placed outdoors or in areas open to children and pets. The housing physically prevents access to the bait except by rodents that can navigate the internal tunnel. Loose bait placement (block bait left without a station housing) is illegal under current EPA rules for most rodenticide formulations and creates secondary poisoning risk for raptors, pets. Wildlife. Every bait station we install is a tamper-resistant housing placed per EPA label requirements.

How many bait stations does my Greensboro property need?

For home homes, 2-6 outside stations placed at the building perimeter, near foundation corners, dumpster areas. Known rodent travel routes, cover most situations. For business homes, the standard is one station per 50-75 linear feet of outside perimeter, plus additional stations at dock entries and dumpster enclosures. We size the station count based on an perimeter walkthrough and document the placement in a site map.

Are bait stations safe around children and pets?

Yes, when correctly specified and placed. Tamper-resistant stations are designed so that children and dogs cannot open them or access the bait inside. We place stations against the building foundation or in areas that limit incidental contact. We never place inside bait stations in living areas or pet-open spaces. We discuss placement relative to your specific household before setups.
