Frequently asked

Common questions

Why are roof rats worse in Irving Park, Fisher Park, and Sunset Hills?

Those three neighborhoods sit under Greensboro's densest mature hardwood canopy, oak and maple trees that have been growing since the early 1900s. Roof rats are arboreal: they live in trees, travel limb-to-limb, and use canopy cover as a protected highway. When a tree limb touches or overhangs a roofline, roof rats move from limb to roof within a day or two. The older homes in these neighborhoods also have original soffit systems, wood gable vents. Aging fascia boards that provide multiple entry points once rats reach the roofline.

How do I know if I have roof rats versus Norway rats in my attic?

Roof rat droppings are banana-shaped with pointed ends, about 1/2 inch long. Norway rat droppings are capsule-shaped with blunt ends, about 3/4 inch. Beyond droppings, roof rats concentrate activity above the ceiling plane, attic scratching that tracks across the ceiling rather than up walls. Norway rats usually enter at ground level and work upward. Roof rats enter at the roofline and work downward. The entry-point location is usually the clearest indicator: soffit gaps and gable-vent openings signal roof rats. Crawl-space vents and foundation gaps signal Norway rats.

Do I need to trim my trees to get rid of roof rats?

Canopy trimming is the most effective long-term prevention measure for roof-rat-prone neighborhoods like Irving Park and Fisher Park. Removing limb-to-roofline contact points breaks the primary access route. We says trimming to maintain at least 6 feet of clearance between tree limbs and any part of the roofline or frame. We don't do tree trimming ourselves. We can identify which specific limbs are creating contact points during the inspections. You can direct an arborist precisely.

Is the roof rat removal process different for historic homes in Greensboro?

Yes. Historic homes in Irving Park, Fisher Park. Sunset Hills needs heritage-friendly exclusion, we avoid drilling or cutting into original architectural elements. Use stainless-steel mesh rather than galvanized hardware cloth where visible (it oxidizes less clearly). Match material to existing vent systems. The goal is sealing the frame against roof rats while preserving the character of the home. It takes longer and costs modestly more, but it's the right method for a Greensboro craftsman from 1920.
