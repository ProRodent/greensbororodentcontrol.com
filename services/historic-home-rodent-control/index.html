Frequently asked

Common questions

What makes rodent exclusion different for historic homes in Irving Park and Fisher Park?

Historic homes in Irving Park and Fisher Park present two distinct challenges. First, the entry-point complexity: early-1900s build has more potential entry points than modern homes, irregular wood framing. Original sill plates that have moved over decades, unique vent configurations. Architectural details like decorative soffits and wood-framed gable vents that don't accept standard exclusion hardware without changes or damage. Second, the material constraint: standard galvanized hardware cloth visible on an original craftsman soffit is an aesthetic problem, we use stainless-steel mesh in visible locations. It oxidizes more slowly and blends better with original finishes. We also avoid drilling into original wood trim, brick. Architectural elements wherever a less invasive attachment method is available.

Is a historic home in Greensboro more likely to have rodent problems?

Yes, a lot. A century of wood movement, multiple generations of plumbing and electrical upgrades, original build tolerances that predate modern pest-exclusion standards. Mature hardwood canopy that grows closer to rooflines every decade all combine to make pre-1945 homes in Greensboro's heritage neighborhoods largely more vulnerable than newer build. Irving Park, Fisher Park, Latham Park, Old Irving Park. Sunset Hills consistently produce the highest-volume roof-rat calls we handle. The crawl-space homes in Aycock, College Hill, and Westerwood dominate Norway rat calls.

Will exclusion work damage the historic character of my Greensboro home?

Not if it's done correctly. The goal is to be invisible, to seal the frame gap without altering the visual character of the home. That means custom-fitted stainless screening at gable vents rather than foam plugs, pipe-collar flashing at plumbing penetrations rather than expanding foam visible from outside. Copper mesh at sill-plate gaps in open crawl spaces rather than hardware cloth stapled to original wood. Written records of every sealed point so any future restoration work knows what was done and where.

Do you work with owners of homes on the National Register or with local historic preservation requirements?

Yes. Rodent exclusion work on the outside of a home in a locally designated historic district in Greensboro may needs a Certificate of Appropriateness from the Greensboro Historic Preservation Commission before certain material are installed. We're familiar with this process. We provide written records of proposed materials and methods before work starts, the kind of records that supports a CoA application. For homes in the Irving Park Historic District or Fisher Park Historic District, we recommend confirming CoA requirements with the city before outside work begins.

For historic home rodent control, the quote is shaped mainly by the materials the original architecture calls for and how many openings need careful, low-impact repair.
